Grocery Store Site Challenge

Some neighbourhoods have no supermarket nearby. They are called food deserts. Your job: find the best place to build a new supermarket, then design it in SketchUp.

1Explore the food deserts about 10 min

A neighbourhood is a food desert when both are true:

1At least 20% of people live in poverty.
2The nearest supermarket is more than 1 mile away.

    Click a red area on the map to see how many people live there.

    2Place your store about 15–20 min

    Move the mouse over the map and the store follows. Watch the numbers in the box on the map change. Click to put it down. Look for empty land or car parks, not houses.

    Fit it to the site: click your store on the map
    • Drag the store to move it.
    • Drag a white dot to make that side longer or shorter.
    • ⟳Drag the orange circle to turn it to line up with the roads.

    3Explain your choice about 10 min

    Write 3–5 sentences. Use numbers from the box on the map. You could start with:

    • We chose this location because…
    • A store here would serve ___ people, including ___ households without a car.
    • This site is better than other places we tried because…
    • One problem with this site is…

    4Create your site plan about 5 min

    First, move the map so it shows your store the way you want it in your document. That view becomes the picture.

    Your site plan has your picture, numbers, reasons and the store size, ready for building it in SketchUp. It is made on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

    Change the rules (teachers)

    About the data and method

    Neighbourhoods: census tracts from the U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey 5-year estimates, via ArcGIS Living Atlas (tables B17020 poverty and B08201 vehicles available). Estimates have margins of error.

    Food stores: USDA SNAP authorized retailers. “Supermarket” means SNAP store type Supermarket or Super Store.

    Transit: U.S. DOT Bureau of Transportation Statistics National Transit Map. 3D buildings: Esri 3D Buildings, ArcGIS Living Atlas.

    Food desert areas are groups of touching food desert neighbourhoods.

    People served assumes people are spread evenly across each neighbourhood. A person counts if they live within the distance rule (1 mile) of your store and are currently more than that distance from any supermarket. Distances are straight-line, not walking routes.

    Minimum plot size is a rule of thumb: 3 × the store floor area, to leave room for parking and deliveries.

    USDA’s official Food Access Research Atlas uses finer grids and more income measures. Results here are for learning, not official designations.
