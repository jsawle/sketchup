Grocery Store Site Challenge

A supermarket chain wants to open one new store to serve a community that lacks healthy food. Use real data to find the food desert, compare three possible sites, and recommend one.

1Choose a community

Move the map anywhere in the United States, then press the button to study your own community.

2Find the food desert

A neighbourhood is a food desert when it fails both of these tests:

1
Low income
At least 20% of people live in poverty.
2
Low access
The nearest supermarket is more than 1 mile away.

Click any neighbourhood on the map to see how it scored on each test. The legend is on the map.

Change the rules (optional)

The standard rule (USDA) is 20% poverty and 1 mile in cities.

3Compare three sites

Three sites are proposed in the most underserved neighbourhoods. For each one:

  1. Fly to it and look at the real buildings and roads.
  2. Move the store onto an empty plot if it lands on buildings.
  3. Rotate and resize it to line up with the roads (height stays 9 m).
  4. Measure the plot. It must be big enough for the store, parking and deliveries.

4Make your recommendation

Your decision is saved as a file on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere and no account is needed.

?Think deeper

  • What does this data not tell you? (Store prices? Opening hours? Safety of the walk?)
About the data and method

Census tracts: U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey 5-year estimates via ArcGIS Living Atlas (tables B17020 poverty, B08201 vehicles available). Estimates have margins of error.

Food stores: USDA SNAP authorized retailer locations. “Full-service” means SNAP store type Supermarket or Super Store.

Transit: U.S. DOT Bureau of Transportation Statistics National Transit Map (stops and routes).

3D buildings: Esri 3D Buildings scene layer, ArcGIS Living Atlas.

Minimum plot size: a rule of thumb of 3 × the store’s floor area, to leave room for customer parking, a delivery yard and landscaping. Real planning rules vary by city.

Simplifications: USDA’s official Food Access Research Atlas measures distance from every half-kilometre grid cell and also uses median family income. This app measures from the centre of each tract, uses the poverty rate only, and counts people in tracts whose centre is within 1 mile of a site. Results are for learning, not official designations.
